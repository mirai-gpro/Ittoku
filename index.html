Cultural Heritage

Treasures Worth ¥300 Million

Each piece was created by master artisans whose lineages have preserved Japan's most sacred cultural sites for generations.

Kano School Tiger Paintings

Originally adorned Nagoya Castle's Audience Chamber (1615). Our replicas were created by the same artisan lineage. Symbols of power in feudal Japan.

Naoe Kanetsugu Samurai Armor

Sengoku period general (1560–1619), famous for his '愛' (Love) helmet ornament. Museum-quality craftsmanship representing one of history's most philosophical samurai.

Imperial Ceiling Art

Peony blossoms symbolizing prosperity and honor. Hand-painted using traditional pigments inspired by Nijo Castle's imperial chambers.

Kinkaku-ji Master Lacquer Floors

Created by artisans who maintain Kyoto's Golden Pavilion. Months of hand-polishing create a mirror-like finish reflecting 400 years of technique.

HORIKIN Gold Leaf Fusuma (Est. 1711)

Each gold leaf individually applied by hand. HORIKIN has adorned imperial palaces and Kyoto temples for over 300 years. No two panels are identical.

Our Hosts

The Heart of Ittoku

At the center of every unforgettable evening at Ginza Ittoku are our hosts: handsome gentlemen impeccably dressed in traditional kimono, who bring warmth, wit, and genuine care to every private experience.

Our cast is proudly gay — and creates a space where every guest is celebrated without reservation. Ittoku is a sanctuary of complete acceptance and elegance, welcoming all who love Japan's beauty.

Fluent in both Japanese and English, our hosts are also passionate storytellers — bringing the 400-year-old cultural treasures surrounding you to life through conversation and genuine human connection.
